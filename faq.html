In the body

ABOT only acts like red blood cells, not the entire blood. To fill the roll of plasma or other platelets, other technologies can be used as well as donations. We evision working on this as well in the future.

The blood types of our current RBC are due to sugars attached to the wall of the cell. Since we are designing ABO, we can ignore this sugar which makes ABOT act like O- blood.

Just like normal RBC, there is an intravenous needle attached to the bag of ABOT with a regulator to ensure correct dosage for the patient.

Normal blood clots due to platelets activating and binding groups of RBC into a sort of barricade. Since ABOT is significantly smaller than RBC, it won’t be able to contribute to clotting. This is both a positive with no blood clots in veins, but also disadvantageous since it won’t be able to help in the clotting of the vein walls. This being said, vein clotting will only be slowed down because ABOT will never represent 100% of a person’s blood.

Administering only RBC is a standard practice in the industry. ABOT will never be administered in an uncontrolled manner though. Just like any other medication, its dosage will be controlled to avoid over-oxygenation of the blood.

ABOT is designed to replicate RBC as nearly as possible and its easily evolvable nature marks it easy to design out any unexpected side effects during trials.

Aptamer-Based Oxygen Transporters (ABOT) will flow through the bloodstream and act with other RBC to deliver oxygen to all parts of the body. It should not have any un-designed interactions with the blood. If this comes up as a problem, we can use the evolution process to correct.

Design and evolution

Aptamers are a very versatile biomedical technology. We are able to evolve them to have certain desired performances. We do this by letting large quantities of them tangled up in different ways while applying mutations, and then using chemical tests to wash away all those that perform the worst. Rinse and repeat until we have desired behavior. This lets us make design changes on the fly and improve the product over the years.

Logistics and distribution

ABOT can actually be stored forever in a dried form which can be later rehydrated and diluted for infusion. This mitigates two of the biggest disadvantages of current blood which are being perishable and needing very controlled conditions (no more than a total of 10 minutes >0C).

ABOT is first and foremost a solution to low blood supplies. That being said, its price will in the future be lower than the price of processing donations of RBC, saving the healthcare industry money. The fact ABOT can also be stored in above 0 temperatures and for longer periods of time than normal RBC means it can also have many interesting applications in developing nations and humanitarian scenarios.

We plan on not directly selling to patients, but instead to partner with blood authorities like Blood Services Canada and hospitals to supply directly to them.
